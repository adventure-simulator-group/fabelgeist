# Furniture form references

Recipes are usable game-space reconstructions for Germany in 1544. Unless a
measurement is explicitly identified below, dimensions are authored placement
envelopes. Compact and broad variants describe scale, not dated museum objects.

- Stuffed mattresses and cushions: Dürer, pillow studies, 1493, Met 1975.1.862.
  [Object record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/459214).
  The separate cover and fitted mattress dimensions are design inferences.
- Socket candlesticks: German fifteenth-century copper-alloy example, Met
  57.135, 0.235 m high and 0.108 m base diameter.
  [Object record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/471832).
  The iron standing support is an authored fixture, not a measured copy.
- Distaff and drop spindle: woodcut after Beham, *Spinning Room*, 1524,
  Rijksmuseum RP-P-OB-21.572.
  [Primary print](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/nl/collectie/object/Spinruimte--b15b9543819fa7f650b739b11d58f99a).
  The stool-mounted stored-tool support is a construction inference.
- Wooden screw press: Cranach title-page border, Wittenberg, 1522, Folger
  218-463q.
  [Book and press description](https://www.folger.edu/explore/collection-highlights/martin-luther-sermon/).
  Bed travel, screw dimensions, and type-case subdivisions are reconstructed;
  no later standardized type-case layout or powered feed is implied.
- Treadle loom: Nürnberg Mendel I, Amb. 317.2°, folio 4 verso, circa 1425.
  [Primary folio](https://online-service.nuernberg.de/viewer/image/5d64f831-7a9d-47b4-9a01-d6a28f29ad99/11/LOG_0011/).
  That image's suspension attaches to the ceiling. The generated independent
  timber suspension frame is an explicit reconstruction for movable placement.
- Beam balances: South German precision scales, 1497, GNM HG11161;
  [object record](https://objektkatalog.gnm.de/objekt/HG11161).
  The larger trade balance is a functional reconstruction, also informed by
  [Adam Kraft's 1497 public-scales relief](https://www.nuremberg.museum/projects/show/166-scales-relief).
- Line reckoning: Adam Ries's surviving 1527 arithmetic edition.
  [Edition record](https://www.adam-ries-bund.de/forschung/erstes/).
  Lines, intermediate spaces, and movable counters carry the numeric operation.

Household contents and equipment selection must follow room use. A press
belongs in a printing house, a loom in a weaver's workshop, and a weighing
balance in commercial work. A shared generic cutting-table kit does not
substitute for these functions.

## Worship fittings

- Octagonal font: surviving late-Gothic font at Mengerschied, circa 1500,
  0.89 m tall and 1.02 m across. The surviving base plate is incomplete.
  [Local object account](https://cms.mengerschied.de/spaetgotischer-taufstein.html).
  The generated octagonal foot, bowl section, and placement are reconstructions.
- Pulpit: the Torgau castle chapel of 1543–1544 supplies a dated example of a
  prominent preaching position. Its court programme is not a universal parish
  template. The generated freestanding stair and platform are authored access
  geometry, not a copy of the Torgau wall attachment.
  [UNESCO site description](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6723/).
- Bima and Torah shrine: Altdorfer's 1519 views document the pre-destruction
  Regensburg synagogue. These support distinct reading and Torah-storage
  functions; the generated joinery and textile folds are reconstruction.
  [Bavarian collection context](https://www.bavarikon.de/object/bav%3ABSB-CMS-0000000000008036?lang=de).
  This reference does not authorize an active Regensburg synagogue in 1544.

Christian and Jewish programmes use separate fittings. A generated synagogue
requires a permitted settlement programme; the furniture catalogue alone does
not establish a community's presence. No invented inscription is applied to
religious furnishings. Bunk beds remain manually selectable catalogue objects
and are not automatically assigned to ordinary bedrooms or guardrooms.

## Wood treatments and upkeep

The Bayerisches Nationalmuseum records a painted wooden bridal chest, MA 2733,
dated around 1500. Its original lid and base are missing. This supports painted
furniture as a period category; it does not establish one universal German
pigment or the appearance of an intact chest in 1544.
[Collection record](https://www.bayerisches-nationalmuseum.de/sammlung/00025152).
The Met's fifteenth-century Westphalian oak chest with iron mounts supports
keeping wood treatment separate from the metal straps.
[Met 13.205.2](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/463632).

Natural, handled, repaired and painted are authored alternatives for tables,
benches, chairs, chests and workbenches. The muted paint color, contact patches,
1.5 mm maximum surface depression, replacement board and room distributions are
reconstruction choices, not measured historical frequencies or museum patina.
Painted bases keep bare working tops. A replacement is one complete existing
board with the same bearing and dimensions; handling changes only the exposed
contact faces. Workshop use favors handled or repaired furniture, independently
of the building's architectural investment.
