# Historical construction references

The curated programmes represent plausible building arrangements for a
fictional Central German settlement in 1544. They are not measured replicas or
claims about the frequency of each form.

## Masonry lower structure and timber upper storeys

The municipal museum's account of Erfurt's Krönbacken describes a stone
building of 1267 carrying a multi-storey timber structure added in 1326, with
an extension into the court in 1428. Its nearby storehouse retains masonry
enclosures and a timber frame and roof dated 1467. These phases support mixed
masonry and timber construction and distinct residential/storage ranges before
1544. The present Renaissance facade and large stair date to the second half
of the sixteenth century and are excluded from this reference.

[Erfurt municipal museums: Patrizierhaus Krönbacken](https://geschichtsmuseen.erfurt.de/gm/de/museen/kroenbacken/index.html)

The merchant-house recipe uses this construction relationship. Its half-metre
lower wall thickness, footprint, projecting upper frame, roof arrangement and
ornament are authored design dimensions; the source does not authenticate
those exact choices. The narrower timber town house and single-storey cottage
remain distinct construction programmes. Building size is not an assertion of
an individual occupant's wealth.

Furniture and movable apparatus have their own
[object and image references](src/furniture/SOURCES.md). Trade sign motifs have
separate [source notes](src/signs/SOURCES.md).

## Merchant courts and rear stores

Wasserburg's municipal historical lexicon describes the front house, court and
rear building at Herrengasse 15. It dates the front structure to around 1453
and a courtyard gallery to 1500. The original use and age of rear masonry are
less certain. Later roof work, the altered rear entrance and its reused
eighteenth-century door are excluded from the procedural reference.

[Wasserburg: Museum building history](https://www.historisches-lexikon-wasserburg.de/Museum)

Lübeck's municipal heritage register identifies medieval courtyard enclosure
walls at An der Mauer 112–114. This northern example supports the existence
of masonry plot boundaries; it does not establish a universal German layout.

[Lübeck heritage register, An der Mauer 112–114](https://bekanntmachungen.luebeck.de/dokumente/d/30/inline#page=104)

The generated merchant property has one front dwelling, a side entrance,
working court and subordinate ground-floor store. Its 9 by 6 metre rear range,
plain gable, boundary dimensions and inward-opening timber gate are authored
choices, not a measured reconstruction. The rear store adds no independent
household or settlement service capacity.

## Bells and timber suspension

Heiden's parish account documents surviving thirteenth-century and 1466 bells,
a timber substructure dated to the 1390s and a bell frame dated to the 1540s.
It distinguishes the present adapted wooden headstocks installed in 2004.
This supports the period use of cast bells and timber carrying structures;
the present fittings are not treated as untouched sixteenth-century evidence.

[Heiden parish: bells and timber frame](https://www.kirche-heiden.de/glocken)

The procedural suspension is an authored mechanical arrangement: two fixed
timber rails carry journals, a timber headstock carries forged straps and a
transverse pin, and open cast crown loops connect the pin to the bell. It is
shown at rest, with an audited clearance envelope of thirty degrees to either
side. This is an authored modest ringing range, not a full-circle ringing
mechanism. Its dimensions and hardware proportions are not a reconstruction
of Heiden's original fittings.
