Verdict: **FAIL** for a faithful coarse representation of the selected object. Confidence: **medium-high (0.8)**. This is a recognizable, anatomically placed close helmet, but the skull-to-nape contour and the balance of skull versus lower face still change the reference's major shape. ## Coherent shape and anatomical placement

The model has a rounded enclosing skull, a separate broad visor with eye slots across the wearer's eyes, a diagonal visor-to-jaw boundary rising toward the temple, an enclosing lower jaw, and a spreading rear neck defense. Those relationships correspond to the photographed object. The front and quarter bare/worn pairs show a helmet of credible overall width around the head and ears. The eye opening is positioned over the eyes, the lower enclosure reaches beneath the chin, and the neck plates extend over the upper back rather than the jaw or shoulders. The face coverage reads as one coherent enclosure rather than an unrelated mask.

The visor sits visually close to the skull at the brow and to the jaw along its lower diagonal edge in side and quarter views. These main boundaries do not show a gross floating separation in the supplied views. The rear plates visibly overlap as a descending sequence. Their separation and concealed interfaces cannot be certified from these images. The bare views are sufficient to judge the principal anatomical locations, but not hidden distances to the wearer.

## Ranked consequential coarse differences

1. **The skull stays too full and upright behind the lower head instead of turning inward clearly below the occiput.** In `diffuse/side`, the rear silhouette descends almost vertically from the back of the cranial dome toward the attachment of the neck plates. The Met side photographs `1483086` and `1483088` show a distinct rounded rear skull that turns forward beneath its bulge into a narrower nape before the neck defense flares outward. `diffuse/rear` similarly reads as an elongated round bowl with a late narrowing, whereas `1483087` has a stronger, longer transition into the neck. The `bare/side` and `bare/rear` wearer images themselves show the head-to-neck narrowing, so its visual loss in the helmet is not established as necessary for this wearer. This changes the main silhouette independently of decorative features.

2. **The dome is more dominant and the lower face/jaw assembly more compressed than in the photographed helmet.** In `diffuse/front` and `diffuse/quarter`, the crown forms a high rounded cap above a comparatively short eye-to-chin enclosure. In reference `1483085` and `main`, the face and lower jaw carry more of the helmet's height and have a more extended, straighter lower enclosure. The candidate's `diffuse/side` also sweeps up under the jaw sooner, making the lower assembly read as a compact chin cup; reference `1483086` and `1483088` carry the jaw volume back into a deeper neck enclosure. The candidate does cover this wearer's chin, so this is a reference proportion difference rather than evidence of exposed anatomy. Different wearer proportions can account for some of it, which makes this a secondary concern rather than the primary reason for failure.

## Reservations and scope

The reference photographs and rendered cameras are not identical, so I am judging persistent silhouette relationships across several views, not matching pixels or extracting dimensions. The reference has no photographed wearer; exact historical internal fit cannot be compared. This assessment does not claim numerical clearance, intersections, mesh integrity, runtime behavior, or support for other bodies. No defect is inferred from metallic highlights, and no omitted surface detail is being used as an acceptance gate. A simpler helmet can satisfy this scope while preserving the reference's main skull-to-nape transition and relative face/jaw volume.
