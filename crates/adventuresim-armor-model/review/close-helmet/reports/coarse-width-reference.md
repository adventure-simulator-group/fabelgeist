# Coarse skull / ear / cheek comparison — candidate 28

**Verdict: FAIL for the coarse-volume match. Confidence: high (0.90).** I actually opened all five Met25397 photographs (front 1483085, three-quarter main/1483089, sides 1483086 and 1483088, rear 1483087) and candidate-28/diffuse front, side, quarter and rear. This analysis uses those images only; it does not judge code, rims, openings, decoration or small surface defects.

## Major silhouette mismatch

**Candidate28 concentrates too much apparent width at the ear/upper-cheek band relative to its upper skull and lower face.** In front, its sides swell outward around the eye/ear region and then turn markedly inward beneath the cheeks into a narrow jaw. In rear, the same low broad cranial lobe drops into a strong inward pinch at the neck. Quarter confirms that the cheek/ear region is a rounded lateral mass, rather than this being only a frontal-camera impression. The overall read is a bulbous middle head over a small lower enclosure.

The reference front is broader more evenly through the upper skull and has substantially straighter descending cheek sides. Its lower face retains appreciable width instead of rapidly collapsing beneath a swollen ear band. The reference rear similarly carries a fuller upper head with a more gradual reduction toward the nape. There is a real neck reduction, but the transition is less like a low, wide ball abruptly pinched underneath. The opposite side photographs show a full rear cranium and a coherent head-to-neck profile; frontal width correction should not sacrifice that depth.

This is principally a **distribution-of-volume problem**, not simply an instruction to reduce the entire helmet's width. Uniform shrinking could leave the same broad-middle/narrow-jaw relationship. Both the level at which the helmet is widest and how long it retains that width matter.

## Meaningful comparison criteria

1. **Normalize the main head envelope.** Compare near-frontal views at matched crown-to-chin height, excluding the comb, lower flange and articulated tail. Do not compare whole-image size or shoulder width between a museum object and a worn render.
2. **Compare the width profile through height.** Inspect upper cranial width, brow width, ear/upper-cheek width, lower-cheek width and chin width together. The decisive relationship is the ear band's breadth relative to the upper skull and lower face, plus the amount and abruptness of narrowing below it. Do not optimize one maximum-width number in isolation.
3. **Require agreement in front and rear, then check quarter and side.** Front should lose the inflated side-cheek mass; rear should not retain a low broad lobe hidden from the front by visor changes. Quarter should read as one coherent cranial enclosure. Side must preserve adequate crown/occiput depth and the face-to-jaw placement rather than flattening the whole helmet to make one camera look narrow.
4. **Separate shape comparison from fit verification.** A matched bare-body front/rear or coronal section is needed to establish where the actual ears and temporal skull sit inside this envelope. The current exterior images establish the silhouette mismatch, not a numerical amount of excess padding or a guarantee that a narrower alternative fits.

No small-feature repair can satisfy this particular review while the broad middle-head proportion remains. A coarse silhouette or plain shaded render is sufficient to reassess it; detail should not determine the verdict. No geometry was edited.
