## Direct comparison

**Met26428:** Its skull/visor/jaw organization is related, but the actual photographed face is a projecting stepped profile above a strong sloping lower wedge. Candidate20 side and quarter instead show a shallow, rounded horizontal visor projecting over a long, relatively upright lower face. The reference's rising lateral boundary and narrowing chin are more emphatic. Plain finish would not remove these geometric differences. The museum identifies some collar lames as modern; they are not used to demand a collar here.

**Met26505:** This is useful evidence that a curved one-piece visor existed, but not proof of candidate20's form. The photograph shows a markedly deeper projecting rounded muzzle, an eye ledge, a shaped brow boundary sweeping around the temple, and a comparatively compact tucked-under chin. Candidate20's visor is shallower, broader in its horizontal-band reading, and sits over a substantially longer upright jaw panel. These differences remain if etching, openwork and fasteners are ignored. The museum also identifies this as a boy's helmet whose openwork visor was unsuitable for dangerous sport; it cannot automatically substantiate a plain combat version.

**Met26508:** The visible visor and upper bevor form distinct projecting steps above the lower chin enclosure. Its lower facial plate boundaries do not support the candidate's single smoothly rounded visor band and long lower panel. Its ornate edges are irrelevant to this mismatch; the arrangement and silhouette remain different after mentally removing decoration. The museum explicitly notes the missing collar lames, so their absence is not treated as evidence for a complete collarless design.

## Consequential mismatches, ranked

1. **Visor profile and frontal volume:** candidate20 side/quarter have a low rounded projection with a largely horizontal brow, while none of the examined real objects has that same combination of projection depth, vertical visor extent, brow boundary and eye treatment.
2. **Jaw/plate proportion:** candidate20 carries a long, comparatively upright jaw enclosure below that shallow visor. The approved reference has a stronger continuous lower wedge; the curved-visor supplementary example has a more compact, tucked chin below a much deeper muzzle. No examined photograph supports the candidate's specific division.

The candidate's rounded skull and narrowing nape are generally coherent with these objects, but those shared features do not resolve the face mismatch. Its wearer relationship remains visually plausible; plausibility cannot supply the missing historical evidence or demonstrate that these differences are required by fit.
