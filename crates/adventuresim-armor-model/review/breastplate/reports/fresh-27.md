## Rounded — UNCERTAIN, medium confidence

The front and quarter views show one broad enclosing convex volume, without anatomical pectoral cups. The lower surface returns toward a short, nearly level waist flange. Relative to the bare body, the shell ends near the flank narrowing and leaves the lower abdomen free. These relationships capture the principal rounded torso and restrained waist of Met 22741. The back is broadly fitted around the shoulder blades and tapers toward the waist.

Consequential unresolved points, ranked:

1. **Upper-shell seating and depth are not convincingly established.** In `rounded/breastplate--worn-side.png`, the upper front runs forward from the collar into a very deep breast volume, leaving a conspicuous wedge of visible chest inside the front arm opening. The body-only side view establishes that much of this depth is designed stand-off. The museum photograph supports an enclosing rounded breast, but its reinforcing and shoulder pieces obscure the corresponding upper boundary. I cannot establish from that photograph whether this particular amount and distribution of stand-off accurately adapts the reference.
2. **The rear armhole transitions need a clearer geometric view.** `rounded/breastplate--worn-rear.png` shows an abrupt pinched-looking dark transition at both lower armhole corners, against otherwise broad smooth back surfaces. The side view also shows a sharply turning lower rear armhole edge, but the arm hides the immediate junction. This is suspicious surface flow, not proof of a fold or intersection from a highlight alone.

The front/back side edges meet closely in the quarter view and their short flanges occupy a common waist band; there is no visually enormous plate separation. The exact overlap is hidden by the arm. A close rear-quarter view with the arm moved clear and a neutral-shaded upper-torso section would resolve the seating reservations. A more exposed historical rounded-shell side photograph would strengthen the reference comparison. I do not call the rounded form a demonstrated mismatch solely because its wearer differs from the mounted museum armor.

## Tapul — UNCERTAIN, high confidence in the evidence limitation

The front and quarter views show a continuous medial ridge dividing two bowed halves, with a shallow central drop into the flange. The shell remains compact vertically and returns inward at the waist. These front-view relationships are consistent with the restrained ridge of Cleveland 1916.1647. Its opening layout is adapted to the broad wearer, and the flank waist position remains coherent against the bare-body views.

Consequential unresolved points, ranked:

1. **The deep rounded side profile is unverified by the target photograph.** `tapul/breastplate--worn-side.png` shows a substantial forward volume through the lower breast, with a broad round return rather than a distinctly angular projecting break. The Cleveland front photograph cannot establish whether that depth and curvature are faithful. The reference list explicitly lacks a verified side photograph for a stronger tapul, so a full shape PASS would exceed the evidence.
2. **Upper opening seating and rear armhole flow retain the rounded style's uncertainty.** The wedge of exposed upper chest is visible in the side image; the pinched-looking rear corners are visible in the rear image. The front/back flank edges appear closely seated in the quarter image, but the relevant junction is partly obscured.

This is not a failure merely for lacking a sharp tapul point: the supplied object is a restrained medial-ridged reference. A corresponding museum side/quarter photograph and an unobscured generated rear-quarter view are the missing evidence needed for acceptance.

## Peascod — FAIL, high confidence

The center ridge is continuous, the upper shell encloses the chest as one volume, and the lower boundary does descend centrally while rising toward the flanks. The waist remains near the wearer's flank narrowing, and the back has the same compact enclosing relationship as the other styles. Nevertheless, the characteristic balance of the supplied peascod objects is not yet represented accurately.

Consequential mismatches, ranked:

1. **The lower point is too modest relative to the broad upper shell.** `peascod/breastplate--worn-front.png` and `breastplate--worn-quarter.png` show a broad shallow V at the lower edge. Worcester's front photograph has a substantially longer, narrowing central descent below its flank waist; Clifford's worn photograph likewise carries the center clearly down between the higher flanks. The generated form reads closer to a rounded ridged breast with a dipped hem than the supplied peascod silhouette. The bare-body views do not demonstrate an anatomical need to suppress this defining descent.
2. **The projecting volume remains bulbous rather than flowing into the low point.** `peascod/breastplate--worn-side.png` shows an extremely prominent rounded belly ending in an almost level forward lip. In the quarter view, the center ridge rounds forward broadly before a short bottom termination. The reference's major visual flow narrows and descends toward its rounded low point; this is a shape-distribution difference, not a demand for a needle tip or separate fauld.

The upper seating and partly hidden flank overlaps retain the uncertainties described above. I would not infer a numerical clearance failure from the large visible volume. Additional profile evidence could refine its intended depth, but it would not remove the clear lower-boundary proportion mismatch established by both supplied photographs.

## Fluted — FAIL, medium confidence

The underlying rounded carrier remains legible, with a broad smooth upper chest and lower waist margin. The grooves follow the main breast curvature and fan outward toward the top. Front, side, and quarter views support that they belong to the surface rather than being detached rods. The short waist flange and broad back retain the rounded style's anatomical relationships.

Consequential mismatches, ranked:

1. **The fluted field reads as a narrow central comb rather than the broad worked belly of the principal reference.** In `fluted/breastplate--worn-front.png` and `breastplate--worn-quarter.png`, narrow, sharply tapering grooves occupy the middle of the breast with large untouched lateral expanses. Cleveland 1916.1640.a has a fuller, more continuous band of channels extending across most of its rounded belly, with visibly rounder channel terminations. The difference concerns the field's shape and integration into the belly, not an exact required channel count or decoration.
2. **The enclosing shell and openings remain incompletely substantiated.** The side image retains the deep upper-breast stand-off and open chest wedge, while the rear image retains the pinched-looking armhole corners. The Met three-quarter photograph supports a rounded projecting breast and narrow waist, but its other pieces obscure these particular boundaries.

The second museum example does show finer fluting, so thin channels alone are not a historical defect. My failure concerns the combined narrow field and pointed isolated channel appearance against the selected bounded-band target. This verdict is less certain than the peascod verdict because channel cross-sections and lighting can change their visual weight. A close neutral-shaded groove view would separate termination geometry from shading. A different independently chosen width/count setting could alter this judgment; the supplied images do not prove those controls are independent or validate their ranges.

## Acceptance boundary

No style receives an unconditional artistic PASS from this evidence. Rounded and restrained medial ridge have plausible main volumes but unresolved seating/reference evidence; peascod has a clear consequential silhouette mismatch; fluted has a more qualified field-shape mismatch. These are visual judgments for this one static generated wearer. I make no claims about mesh integrity, exact thickness or clearance, all-body support, animation, runtime installation, or numerical parameter independence.
