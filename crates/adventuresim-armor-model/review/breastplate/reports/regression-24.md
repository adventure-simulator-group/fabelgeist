# Candidate 24: independent known-failure regression review

**PASS for the coarse fit and proportion regressions, moderate confidence, for rounded, tapul, peascod, and fluted.** This is a bounded visual verdict for the supplied static wearer and four designs, not a claim of complete historical fidelity or production readiness.

Actually viewed all 16 worn `front`, `side`, `quarter`, and `rear` images in `target/breastplate-review/24/{rounded,tapul,peascod,fluted}/renders`, plus `target/breastplate-review/24/rounded/sections.png`. The sections identify actual blue plate walls and brown body at waist y=1.17 m, lower flank y=1.22 m, upper flank y=1.28 m, and midline x=0. The shared rear assessment is supported by the matching rear and side renders of all four styles. I did not read source, numerical audit results, or another critic's verdict.

The actual assembled shape now reads as a compact front shell and a comparatively close rear shell around one torso. Its waist and rear contour are coherent with the sectioned wearer; the lateral returns are local construction features rather than a second detached torso volume. The rounded and fluted fronts remain single enclosing volumes, while the ridged designs visibly organize the same basic torso coverage around a central edge and different lower-point relationships.

1. **Long wall, rear bulb, and excessive lower flare: absent at coarse scale.** The front and rear hems sit in the waist region and leave the pelvis below them. `Side` and the midline section show a smooth rear profile with a small lower return, not the former upper-back balloon, deep pinch, and outward hanging panel. The transverse sections show close posterior enclosure with room across the lumbar hollow. Requiring the rigid back to copy that hollow exactly would confuse anatomical shape with useful plate seating.
2. **Disconnected lateral returns: resolved at the reviewed scale.** Waist and flank sections show the two shells wrapping around the body with narrow local spacing and a rear return outside the front ends. `Quarter` still shows an exposed lateral strip, but it now meets the lower front/flange region as a coherent adjacent plate boundary. `Side` no longer exposes the old widening bare waist wedge. A visible fastening interval is not itself a failure; no missing hardware is invented as a new requirement.
3. **Angular upper-arm notch and front-style regressions: substantially resolved.** In `front` and `quarter`, the shoulder/arm boundary now curves around the deltoid instead of presenting the previous abrupt angular bite beneath the shoulder tip. Shoulder tops and the neckline have rounded transitions. Tapul and peascod retain continuous medial edges and the peascod retains a distinct lower center rather than a broad U apron. The fluted band is compact and nearly vertical, with broad smooth margins and no return of the long wavy field.

Reservation: both rear axillary edges have a small localized dark crease/dimple in `rear`; the `side` view places this in the transition around the arm opening. It is not the old broad rear-volume defect, and I would not fail the coarse-shape milestone for it based on this shading alone. A close diagnostic-shaded rear-quarter view would distinguish a local surface crease from a lighting effect if that region is refined further. The arm hides part of the upper lateral seam, so this is not a proof of continuous coverage or clearance there.
