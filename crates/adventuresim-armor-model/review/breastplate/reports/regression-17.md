# Candidate 17: independent known-failure regression review

**PASS for the coarse seating regressions, moderate confidence, for rounded, tapul, peascod, and fluted.** This is a bounded regression verdict on the supplied wearer and static shapes, not complete historical or production acceptance. Candidate 18 was not inspected.

Actually viewed all 16 worn images in `target/breastplate-review/17/{rounded,tapul,peascod,fluted}/renders/breastplate--worn-{front,side,quarter,rear}.png`, plus `target/breastplate-review/17/rounded/sections.png`. The latter shows actual blue plate walls and the brown body at waist, lower flank, upper flank, and midline. The coordinator identifies the rear as shared by all four styles; the matching worn rear/side views corroborate that relationship. No source or numerical audit results were used.

- **Large rear cavity and independent lower flare: resolved at coarse scale.** The midline section now shows a smooth rear carrier following the upper-back-to-waist trend, with a restrained lower return. The previous pronounced hollow followed by a detached outward flare is absent. Waist and flank sections show a relatively close posterior enclosure rather than the broad void of candidate 09. `Rear` is correspondingly smooth; `side` no longer reads as an inflated upper back pinched into a separate hanging lower panel. Some deliberate clearance remains, especially around the lumbar hollow, which is reasonable for a smooth rigid shell rather than a skin-tight anatomical copy.
- **Front/back lateral relationship: acceptable for the simple shell milestone.** In the waist section the back wraps radially outside the front's ends, with a modest lateral interval; the lower flank has a similar overlapping angular arrangement. At the upper flank the separation is more apparent near the arm opening. The worn `quarter` views still expose the return strip, but the sections establish that it belongs to a close rear shell and that the waist no longer has the earlier broad unbridged wedge between receding panels. A visible adjustable fastening interval does not itself require failure, and excluded straps or buckles are not demanded here. This is not a claim that the seam is sealed or that the plates contact. The remaining separation reads as local construction spacing rather than a gross torso-proportion defect.
- **Earlier front-shape regressions remain absent.** `Front` and `quarter` preserve a short waist-level shell and restrained flange. Tapul and peascod retain continuous medial edges; peascod has a distinct central drop with higher flanks rather than the former U-shaped apron. Fluted `front`, `quarter`, and `side` show a compact, nearly vertical channel band with smooth upper and lower margins; the earlier long wavy field has not returned. The flutes remain subordinate to a single rounded front volume.

Anatomical placement: the shoulder openings leave the deltoids exposed and the upper tips remain medial to them. The rear no longer makes an exaggerated second torso behind the wearer. Waist sections and worn views show the shell ending around the narrower torso region instead of continuing as a long barrel over the pelvis. The angular arm-opening transitions are visible, but do not establish a consequential coarse seating failure in this pose.

Reservations: the front remains deliberately well forward of the body, which the midline section makes explicit; exact historical projection is still outside this regression pass. I actually viewed Met 22741 earlier, but current Cleveland/Worcester museum photographs have not been available through this reviewer's browser, so this report does not certify exact museum-object correspondence for those presets. The upper flank/arm-opening region is partly hidden by the arm in the worn views. The single rounded section set supports a shared rear assessment, not exact front clearance for the other presets. No numerical clearance, winding, thickness, self-intersection, morph-range, flute-count/width-range, animation, or runtime claim follows from this visual pass.
