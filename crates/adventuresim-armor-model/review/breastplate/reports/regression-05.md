# Candidate 05: independent known-failure regression review

Scope: simple front and back shells with an integral flange; rounded, medial-ridged/tapul, peascod, and fluted presets. Straps, lance rest, decoration, and separate hip defenses are excluded. This is a regression review, not the fresh broad reference verdict.

Viewed all 32 supplied images: `target/breastplate-review/05/{rounded,tapul,peascod,fluted}/renders/breastplate--worn-{front,side,quarter,rear}.png` and each camera-matched `-body.png`. I previously actually viewed the Met 22741 photograph (https://collectionapi.metmuseum.org/api/collection/v1/iiif/22741/60375/main-image) during candidate 01 review. Current museum-image viewing could not be completed: browser inventory was empty, both the existing browser and direct in-app tab creation reported unavailable, and web fallback did not display the required photos. Cleveland object text was accessible, but is not treated as viewed photographic evidence. Consequently, no new exact museum-photo match is certified here.

## Findings shared across the four presets

1. **Widening lateral panel gap: improved but still present.** Each `side` shows a bare flank wedge between the front and back below the elbow, widening to the lower edge. Each `quarter` shows the rear edge as a narrow separated strip behind the front side edge. This is appreciably smaller than rounded 01, but the boundary still reads as an open interval rather than a close construction seam or overlap. The lower panel spacing remains the consequential unresolved seating regression. Adding omitted straps would not itself change this coarse relationship.
2. **Long, weak-waisted lower shell: substantially resolved.** `front`, `quarter`, and `rear` now show a clear contraction before the short flange, while the body-matched views locate that contraction in the wearer's narrower abdominal/waist region. The former long straight lower barrel has gone. Rounded and fluted preserve a full convex front; tapul returns more forcefully below its forward bulge; peascod deliberately descends centrally while its flanks remain high. I would not continue failing the entire family for candidate 01's excessive lower length.
3. **Forward projection: body uncertainty resolved, historical amount still uncertain.** Matched `side-body` views establish that the shell's large front volume is designed standoff from this comparatively slim torso, rather than the wearer's underlying belly. It is a substantial projection, especially the rounded/fluted mid-front, tapul's concentrated lower-chest bulge, and peascod's low front. That observation is not numerical clearance or proof of an inappropriate historical amount. A suitable reference profile or section comparison is still needed before accepting its magnitude. The anterior neck edge also visibly stands away from the upper chest in `side`; padding/strap exclusions do not establish the intended seating distance.

## Per-style verdicts

- **Rounded: FAIL, moderate confidence.** The old waist/length defect is substantially absent, and the single rounded breast remains coherent. The residual lower side gap remains visible in `side` and `quarter`. Exact projection acceptance remains uncertain for the reason above.
- **Tapul: FAIL, moderate confidence.** Clear waist return improves the prior lower-shell problem, but the shared side gap remains. `front` and `quarter` show a smooth central swelling with a soft, nearly absent upper medial ridge; the image evidence available here cannot certify its correspondence to the intended Cleveland object. This photographic limitation is separate from the visible gap failure.
- **Peascod: FAIL, moderate confidence.** `front` and `quarter` now show a coherent low central drop and higher side waist; `side` shows the intentionally low forward volume. The old uniformly low cylindrical hem is absent. The residual side interval remains, and exact historical depth and ridge definition remain unaccepted without the unavailable museum photographs.
- **Fluted: FAIL, moderate confidence for seating; promising fluting appearance.** The underlying rounded shell shares the lower side-gap failure. The visible channels in `front`, `quarter`, and `side` belong to the surface, fan around the chest, and taper into smooth upper and lower margins; they do not look like detached rods. The channels have a noticeable outward-and-inward bend around the breast, whose exact historical correspondence remains uncertain without viewing the designated fluted object. A single fluted configuration cannot establish that flute count and width adjustments behave correctly.

These verdicts establish neither winding/topology integrity, numerical clearance, self-intersection status, supported morph coverage, parameter-range behavior, nor runtime acceptance.
