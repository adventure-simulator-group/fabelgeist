## Ranked consequential findings

1. **Shared rear shell has an unexplained bulb, lumbar contraction, and large outward skirt.** In each worn-side view, the back projects substantially behind the shoulder-blade region, turns inward abruptly above the waist, then reverses into a long outward flare. The matching bare-body side view has a much gentler continuous back-to-waist contour. The rear views confirm a broad horizontal contraction running across the shell, followed by an outward lower region; this is corroborated by the side silhouette, not inferred from a highlight alone. The quarter views show the lower side edge kicking outward as an independent hanging strip. A short integral flange is appropriate, but this larger reverse-curved rear volume does not read as a restrained shell seated around this wearer's waist. The supplied historical front/quarter references do not establish such a rear form. This is consequential for all four presets.

2. **Front/back seating at the flank is unresolved and looks too open in the available quarter views.** In all four worn-quarter images, the visible rear-shell lower side edge stands away from the front shell, with a wedge of background visible between them. The front images also show the rear edge outside the front waist boundary. This is evidence of a visibly separated relationship, but the arm obscures the upper join and the oblique view does not establish the true shortest separation or whether another part overlaps out of sight. I cannot certify a construction-scale overlap from these images. This reservation applies to all four presets; it is not a numerical-clearance or intersection claim.

3. **Fluted preset's channel field changes direction more conspicuously than the reference band.** In `fluted` worn-front and worn-quarter, the outer channels bend inward on their upper approach and form a domed field around the breast prominence. Cleveland 1916.1640.a instead presents a more transverse bounded band, with a modest outward fan toward the upper ends and broad smooth margins. The model does retain a coherent underlying shell and smooth margins, and the channels do not read as separate attached rods. The mismatch is the field's overall flow and termination pattern, not an objection to adjustable flute count or width. This is secondary to the shared rear-shell issue.

## Preset-specific shape assessment

**Rounded:** The front is a single broad convex volume without anatomical pectoral cups. Front and quarter show a coherent transition into a level, short lower edge; side shows a rounded projection returning toward the waist. These are compatible with the coarse front character of Met 22741, adapted to a broader wearer. The neck opening sits below the neck base and the arm openings leave the deltoids exposed. The reference's reinforcing components limit an exact opening comparison. I do not treat the large front projection alone as a proven defect: a globose enclosing volume is intentional in this family. Acceptance nevertheless fails on finding 1, with finding 2 unresolved.

**Tapul:** Front and quarter clearly show two gently bowed halves organized by a continuous medial ridge and a shallow central lower-edge drop. It remains compact rather than developing a pendant peascod point. That is a credible coarse relationship to Cleveland 1916.1647. Its strongly projecting side profile is not independently established by the supplied frontal museum image; the name “tapul” cannot supply the missing profile evidence. Acceptance fails on finding 1, with finding 2 and historical profile depth unresolved.

**Peascod:** Front and quarter show a lower central termination with the bottom rising toward the flanks, and side shows the prominence moved downward relative to the rounded form. The point remains part of the main shell rather than a detached spike. This supports the intended family, although the generated front is broader and less emphatically tapered than Worcester's isolated shell and the worn Met silhouette. The broad wearer can justify some breadth, but hidden waist anatomy does not prove that all the additional depth is required. I leave that depth judgment unresolved instead of declaring a defect from impression alone. Acceptance fails on finding 1, with finding 2 unresolved.

**Fluted:** The rounded carrier, short level waist edge, exposed shoulders, and bounded surface channels remain legible together in front, side, and quarter. This supports the rounded/fluted family, but the field's upper flow differs as described in finding 3. Acceptance fails on finding 1, with finding 2 unresolved. These images show one fluted example; they do not demonstrate that flute count and width are independently controllable across the requested parameter range.
