# German wool gown and deferred construction

`german1544-outfit.json` describes one restrained wool gown for a German setting
around 1544. It uses a broad square neckline, fitted sleeves, a natural waist
and an unhooped skirt with regular pleats. The oxblood color and exact fiber,
locale and social interpretation are design choices, not identified pigments or
provenance. This is a gown asset, not a complete historical street outfit or an
exact reconstruction of a surviving garment.

## Historical comparisons

- Hans Sebald Beham's *Peasant's Feast*, also called the *Twelve Months*,
  1546-1547, LACMA 61.41.3-.12, supplies a near-date German comparison for the
  restrained fitted upper section and full skirt.
  [Museum record](https://collections.lacma.org/object/63472).
- *Portrait of Sibylla Kessel*, about 1540-1545, Stadel Museum, inventory 969,
  supplies a wealthier Cologne comparison. Its fur and jewelry are not part of
  this gown.
  [Museum record](https://sammlung.staedelmuseum.de/en/work/portrait-of-sibylla-kessel).

These sources support broad design choices. They do not resolve every hidden
seam or certify the exact wool, dye or underclothes. No source photograph is
distributed with the recipe.

## Construction contract

The fitted torso and optional sleeves use one retained anatomical surface.
The hanging skirt surrounds both legs and shares its waist boundary with the
upper cloth. Native body ancestry, source topology and skin remain fixed across
refits; body geometry is evaluated in the canonical unposed, Y-up metre frame.
The sleeve endpoint is a retained reference cut, not a fresh plane recut for
every morph. Sleeve reach spans 40 to 98 percent of shoulder-to-wrist length.
Full wrist coverage is withheld because fitting at the hand transition did not
converge consistently; hand and finger topology is outside this construction.

Fitted sleeves require a fitted torso and no lower equipment layers. Current
lower-garment contact admission does not cover arbitrary sleeved underclothes.
A separate chemise cannot be inferred from an edge strip. Solid cloth uses the
bodice material chart on the arms; this is not a separate cylindrical sleeve
chart. Extend chart ownership before claiming general cuff-band alignment.

Keep broad silhouette folds regular. Future cloth simulation may produce
irregular drape. Fine ornament belongs in parametric color and height-derived
normal maps. Count both sides, edge walls and hidden sheets against the normal
10,000-triangle article budget. Generated-pose clipping remains a defect;
provisional animation skinning does not waive it.

## Deliberately deferred features

The merge scope excludes free and supported paned sleeves, compression bands,
bagging and lobes; independently placed square underarm gussets and the
unaccepted shared-carrier gusset prototype; independent free-volume sleeve caps,
expanded cuffs, cuff binding and backing, slit and frilled sleeve treatments;
turned gown collars and their supporting sheets; independent torso inserts;
and geometric gown neckline binding. Their constructors and controls are
removed, rather than exposed as unaccepted alternatives.

The earlier multilayer Cranach outfit is not a supported shipped reconstruction.
The general skirt puff-and-slash system, reusable textile patterns and separate
partlet APIs are distinct features. Their existence does not certify interaction
with this fitted-sleeve gown.

## Advice for future engineers

Start with coarse volumes and shared seam ownership. Add ornament and hidden
supports after those relationships work. Preserve source winding guards, fixed
morph correspondence and generated-pose intersection checks.

Observed failure mechanisms:

- Moving an ideal square gusset independently moved its seam without moving the
  surrounding cloth. The carrier folded before final seating. Rotating the same
  independently relocated seam does not restore its missing relationship.
- Global projected body hulls admitted remote pelvis and shoulder geometry.
  Frozen local correspondence improved ownership but is not a continuous
  collision certificate.
- Global and local miter pre-offsets reversed source facets. Do not weaken the
  winding guard to retain those offsets.
- Torso-axis displacement dropped medial-arm contacts. Local native directions
  fixed that measured case, not arbitrary layered construction.
- More puff intervals and bypassing final seating did not remove the angular
  sleeve cage. Bypassing seating made clipping worse.
- A continuous fullness direction can remove piecewise-normal jumps while
  exposing an earlier carrier fold. A positive outward dot product alone does
  not establish the material's physical clearance reserve.
- Width-preserving ribbons and anchors at both band lips improved individual
  support relationships without establishing an accepted complete paned sleeve.
- A monotone axial map preserved cuff dimensions but still let a separately
  constructed cap intersect the bodice under the arms.

Fold-only render-row decimation is also withheld. Clear vertices and a bounded
fold-amplitude error did not stop the intervening skirt triangles from cutting
through a supported hip shape. Keep explicit axial body-shape sampling and
validate the emitted facets; do not reinstate that reduction using only a
square-root fold error estimate.

Promising approaches that were not completed as full solutions:

- Prepare neighboring panels around one shared seam. Couple their rest pattern
  and fitted surface instead of relocating attachment points independently.
- Relax added interior fullness while fixing the seam, distributing area through
  neighboring cloth rather than displacing the boundary in isolation.
- Separate physical gauge reserve from the smooth direction used for artistic
  fullness. Check adaptive or continuous contact witnesses on the final surface.
- Give loose sleeves their own prepared chart and coupled armhole contract.
  Extend lower-layer contacts to both arms before admitting sleeved underlayers.

The broad Dressmaker comparison suggests prepared compatible panels and shared
fitting as useful architecture. The inspected material did not demonstrate a
general cloth solver or arbitrary layered-garment guarantee. The implementation
must remain independent of proprietary code and assets.
