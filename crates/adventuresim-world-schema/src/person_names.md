
## Historical scope and evidence

The catalog serves Christian Germans in the 1544 playable region
(8.965–11.110 E, 50.877–52.211 N). It combines Low German and Central German
forms. This is a regional approximation shared across the map, not a dialect
classifier or a separate Lutheran baptismal repertoire.

Sources:

- Sara L. Uckelman, *Low German Names from Hamburg, 1475–1529* (2006):
  <https://www.ellipsis.cx/~liana/names/german/hamburg.html> and
  <https://www.ellipsis.cx/~liana/names/german/hamburgsur.html>.
  Supplies dated northern given-name forms and the northern surname selection.
  Hamburg is outside the playable region. The author explicitly declines to
  infer frequencies because repeated records can concern the same person.
- Kai Lehmann, *Leben und Sterben vor, während und nach dem Dreißigjährigen
  Krieg in der Gemeinde Fambach (1559–1703)*, counts for 1559–1639 reproduced at
  <https://www.beliebte-vornamen.de/1680-vornamen-im-16-jahrhundert.htm>.
  Supplies the Central German frequency reference and seven surnames (Heller,
  Weihrauch, Weih, Siebert, Sittig, Zeis, Heimel). It is later than the setting
  and outside the playable bounds; its baptismal counts are not frequencies of adults alive in 1544.
- Sara L. Uckelman, *German Names from 1495*, drawn from Peter Schmid's
  edition of German Order tax records (2000):
  <https://www.ellipsis.cx/~liana/names/german/german1495.html> and surname tables
  <https://www.ellipsis.cx/~liana/names/german/surnames1495a-g.html>,
  <https://www.ellipsis.cx/~liana/names/german/surnames1495h-m.html>,
  <https://www.ellipsis.cx/~liana/names/german/surnames1495n-s.html>,
  <https://www.ellipsis.cx/~liana/names/german/surnames1495t-z.html>.
  Supplies broader given-name attestations and repeated surname spellings.
  Much of this material is southern German, outside the playable bounds.
- Brian M. Scott (Talan Gwynek), extracts from Roland Mulch's *Arnsburger
  Personennamen* (1974):
  <https://www.s-gabriel.org/docs/german15f.html> and
  <https://www.s-gabriel.org/names/talan/germmasc/arnsburg15.html>.
  Supplies fifteenth-century Hessian names, including rare feminine forms.
- Brian M. Scott, extracts from Volkmar Hellfritzsch's *Vogtländische
  Personennamen* (1969):
  <https://www.s-gabriel.org/names/talan/germmasc/plauen15.html> and
  <https://www.s-gabriel.org/names/talan/germmasc/plauen16.html>.
  Supplies masculine names from the fifteenth century and the 1501–1550
  column only. These are first-attestation windows, not frequencies; the last
  window extends six years beyond the setting. Plauen is also outside the map.
- Hessen's dialect overview:
  <https://landwirtschaft.hessen.de/heimat/mundart/mundartlandkarte>.
  Establishes the North Hessian/Low German boundary as context for the mixed
  repertoire, not a reconstruction of the precise linguistic map in 1544.

## Adaptation

All catalog weights are **authored relative sampling weights**, not historical
population estimates. The female leading weights retain the Fambach ratios;
other weights broaden that sample with northern names and reduce dependence on
one later parish. The male weights retain a dominant Hans/Johann family while
raising Heinrich, Konrad, Nikolaus and Hermann for the northern repertoire.
Those adjustments are editorial estimates, not measured regional corrections.
Surnames are equally weighted. The 1495 tables supply occurrence counts,
but these are not usable as frequencies for the MVP region. Additional northern
surnames retain unannotated headwords, excluding flagged possessives, relational
phrases and the descriptive Aldus. The broader 1495 selection retains spellings
with at least two occurrences, no editorial note, and a capitalized single-word
alphabetic form. Spellings already present are not duplicated. This reproducible
filter excludes explicitly marked feminine forms and uncertainties, but does
not establish local prevalence or merge all surname spelling variants.

The enlarged repertoire preserves the original common-family weights and adds
a low-weight tail of period names. Rare Hessian forms receive particularly low
weights. Name variety therefore does not imply uniform given-name frequency.
The broad surname pool is a German repertoire approximation; it has no regional
selection or inferred dialect probabilities.

Each family receives its weight once. Its listed display forms divide that
weight equally; these splits are also authored. Hans/Johann and other variants
therefore do not become more common merely because more forms are listed.
Historical short forms are kept where useful, but inflected documentary forms
and Latin oblique-case forms are omitted. Attested nominative forms such as
Johannes, Hinricus, Paulus and Wilhelmus remain available. Language/register
selection is not implemented: these forms currently share their family weight
with vernacular forms. Readable normalizations include
Herman → Hermann, Katherina/Catharina → Katharina, Alheyden → Adelheid,
Gertraud → Gertrud, Symon → Simon, Affra → Afra, Fronicka → Veronica,
Walpurg → Walburga, Eyda → Ida, Mechttelt → Mechthild, Hiltegundis → Hildegund,
Anthonius → Antonius, Bernhart → Bernhard, and Friderich → Friedrich.
Other short forms with uncertain expansions remain independent entries rather
than acquiring speculative etymological groupings. Metke is retained without
asserting a certain expansion; its source labels the derivation uncertain.

The sampler consumes deterministic entropy without machine-width-dependent
reduction. Updating this catalog changes newly generated display names and
sign brands; it does not rewrite persisted characters or their identifiers.
