
## Historical scope and evidence

The catalog serves Christian Germans in the 1544 playable region
(8.965–11.110 E, 50.877–52.211 N). It combines Low German and Central German
forms. This is a regional approximation shared across the map, not a dialect
classifier or a separate Lutheran baptismal repertoire.

Sources:

- Sara L. Uckelman, *Low German Names from Hamburg, 1475–1529* (2006):
  <https://www.ellipsis.cx/~liana/names/german/hamburg.html> and
  <https://www.ellipsis.cx/~liana/names/german/hamburgsur.html>.
  Supplies dated northern given-name forms and the surnames through Wreden.
  Hamburg is outside the playable region. The author explicitly declines to
  infer frequencies because repeated records can concern the same person.
- Kai Lehmann, *Leben und Sterben vor, während und nach dem Dreißigjährigen
  Krieg in der Gemeinde Fambach (1559–1703)*, counts for 1559–1639 reproduced at
  <https://www.beliebte-vornamen.de/1680-vornamen-im-16-jahrhundert.htm>.
  Supplies the Central German frequency reference and surnames Heller through
  Heimel. It is later than the setting and outside the playable bounds; its
  baptismal counts are not frequencies of adults alive in 1544.
- Hessen's dialect overview:
  <https://landwirtschaft.hessen.de/heimat/mundart/mundartlandkarte>.
  Establishes the North Hessian/Low German boundary as context for the mixed
  repertoire, not a reconstruction of the precise linguistic map in 1544.

## Adaptation

All catalog weights are **authored relative sampling weights**, not historical
population estimates. The female leading weights retain the Fambach ratios;
other weights broaden that sample with northern names and reduce dependence on
one later parish. The male weights retain a dominant Hans/Johann family while
raising Heinrich, Konrad, Nikolaus and Hermann for the northern repertoire.
Those adjustments are editorial estimates, not measured regional corrections.
Surnames are equally weighted: neither source supplies usable regional counts.

Each family receives its weight once. Its listed display forms divide that
weight equally; these splits are also authored. Hans/Johann and other variants
therefore do not become more common merely because more forms are listed.
Historical short forms are kept where useful, but inflected documentary forms
and Latin grammatical endings are omitted. Readable normalizations include
Herman → Hermann, Katherina/Catharina → Katharina, Alheyden → Adelheid,
Gertraud → Gertrud, and Symon → Simon. Metke is retained without asserting a
certain expansion; its source labels the derivation uncertain.

The sampler consumes deterministic entropy without machine-width-dependent
reduction. Updating this catalog changes newly generated display names and
sign brands; it does not rewrite persisted characters or their identifiers.
