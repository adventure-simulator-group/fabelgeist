# Privacy scope and external anchors

The packet is source and audit evidence, not a representation that publication
is automatically safe. A recursive digest scan uses a 40-digest corpus derived
from restricted prior GPU evidence and bounded nested views; a separate flat
pattern scan checks home/temporary paths, Windows user paths, email-like and
credential-like forms. Preliminary results are preserved with the sealed
packet; the final scan is stored as a separate receipt so it can bind the exact
final manifest without a self-reference. The preliminary scans found zero of
the corpus's forbidden digests, zero email-like patterns and zero credential-like
patterns. They found three local-home and fourteen temporary-path pattern hits;
these are retained in audit locator/incident records and must be reviewed or
redacted before public release. The exact accidental write record is private
operational evidence, not source or a source-read claim.

Scans are bounded by input size and recursion depth; they do not clear unknown
secrets, unsupported encodings or nested formats, symlinks, unexamined Git
history or external inputs. They do not judge meaning, independent custody,
truth or runtime behavior. Raw commit objects may include personal metadata,
the full historical queues may contain private content, and the 95e ledger
snapshot may evolve. These external anchors are deliberately outside the packet
and are referenced by SHA-256, not declared safe to publish. Keep their custody
and exact identities with the central evidence collection.
