# Heraldic source artwork

These are unmodified Wikimedia Commons SVG downloads, retained as drawing
sources. Their digital fill colors are part of the modern artwork, not
measurements or specifications for historical materials. There is no paint
implementation or prescribed material palette in this asset collection.

## German lion

`German_Lion_1530.svg` is [*Lion Rampant Or (16th century German)*](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lion_Rampant_Or_(16th_century_German).svg)
by Tom Lemmens (Tom-L), uploaded 28 September 2013, after Rinaldum's
[*Héraldique meuble lion rampant 02*](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:H%C3%A9raldique_meuble_lion_rampant_02.svg),
dated 8 December 2009. Rinaldum also offers GFDL; this copy uses CC BY-SA 3.0.

The historical model is [BSB Cod.icon. 391](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb00007681),
*Sammelband mehrerer Wappenbücher*, southern Germany (Augsburg?), about 1530.
The Commons source points to fol. 177r (scan 355); the SVG document name
instead identifies fol. 286v (scan 574). Preserve that uncertainty rather than
assigning an unqualified folio. The modern redraw is not a color facsimile.

## Wernigerode eagles

The drawings are by Tom Lemmens (Tom-L) and Heralder:

- `Wernigerode_single_eagle.svg`: [*Arms of the King of the Romans
  (c.1433-1486)*](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Arms_of_the_King_of_the_Romans_(c.1433-1486).svg),
  drawing dated 6 January 2014.
- `Wernigerode_double_eagle.svg`: [*Arms of the Holy Roman Emperor
  (c.1433-c.1450)*](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Arms_of_the_Holy_Roman_Emperor_(c.1433-c.1450).svg),
  drawing dated 15 October 2013. Downloaded Commons revision of 30 December
  2023, restoring the revision of 17 December 2013.

The double eagle traces the *Wernigeroder (Schaffhausensches) Wappenbuch*,
southern Germany, about 1475–1500, Bayerische Staatsbibliothek,
[Cod.icon. 308 n](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb00043104),
[scan 23](https://api.digitale-sammlungen.de/iiif/image/v2/bsb00043104_00023/full/1200,/0/default.jpg).
The modern artists made it symmetrical and changed proportions. Their redraw
replaces small head crowns with halos. The single eagle is a modern variation
in this style, not an independently traced single-headed period specimen.
Dates in the artwork titles describe the represented arms, not the drawing.
